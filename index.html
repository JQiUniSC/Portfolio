About

I am a PhD candidate in neurobiology with a strong commitment to independent and rigorous scientific research. My work is characterised by a detail-oriented and analytical approach, supported by strong problem-solving skills. My primary research interests include cell division, protein–protein interactions, cellular differentiation, and neurobiology, while maintaining a broader interest in the biological sciences.

In addition to my research activities, I have extensive teaching experience spanning over a decade, having taught students both domestically and internationally across a range of subjects and academic levels. I am committed to fostering effective learning through structured, interactive, and student-centred teaching approaches.

Research & Projects

Current project: Unlocking the proteomics of Glycine receptors

This project explores the interaction of glycine receptors with auxiliary subunits.

These subunits regulate receptor function. My current work focuses on identifying interaction networks and understanding how glycine receptors are controlled using proteomics and biochemical assays.

Substrate stiffness & neural crest EMT

This project examined how substrate stiffness affects cell migration.

The aim was to build a reproducible model showing how mechanical properties influence EMT and migration.

HOW: The Long and short of intestinal stem cells

This project looked at a gene called HOW and its expression in intestinal stem cells.

HOW expression was shown to impact differentiation and proliferation of stem cells.
