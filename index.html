About

Research & Projects

Current project: Unlocking the proteomics of Glycine receptors

This project explores the interaction of glycine receptors with auxiliary subunits.

These subunits regulate receptor function. My current work focuses on identifying interaction networks and understanding how glycine receptors are controlled using proteomics and biochemical assays.

Substrate stiffness & neural crest EMT

This project examined how substrate stiffness affects cell migration.

The aim was to build a reproducible model showing how mechanical properties influence EMT and migration, using controlled substrates and microscopy techniques. Prior research shown by Barriga et al. (2018) showed that underlying tissue stiffening caused cells to migrate while softening lead to both arrest in cell migration as well as differentiation. This project was designed to look at producing a more sustainable and useful model for research and further testing as the previous research was conducted on the African clawed frog. By reproducing this on a human cell culture model, many confounding factors can be eliminated when further research is conducted.

HOW: The Long and short of intestinal stem cells

This project looked at a gene called HOW (Held out wing) and its expression in intestinal stem calls of fruit flies.

HOW expression in the intestinal stem cells of fruit flies was shown to impact differentiation of cells. Inhibition of HOW lead to a lower amount of intestinal stem cells as well as inhibiting the amount of cells it differentiated towards. The 2 isoforms that were expressed were also shown to have varying effects with the longer isoform leading to more intestinal stem cells in the gut as well as far more proliferation and differentiation. Results from this research could help answer some of the ways in which intestinal cancer is able to be spread as a similar gene is expressed in humans.
